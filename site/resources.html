Study further in Australia

These Australian university programs and research centres informed the scope of this course and offer routes into deeper study. Check each institution's current offerings, as programs and subjects change between years.

Griffith University

Australian Research Centre for Human Evolution (ARCHE)

Research centre whose members have worked on Sulawesi cave art, the archaeology of Flores and dating methods. Relevant to Modules 2–4.

Research centre Australian National University

Human Evolution specialisation

A structured undergraduate specialisation in human evolution, from an institution with a long history in Australian archaeology, including the Lake Mungo research.

Degree specialisation University of Melbourne

ZOOL30004 (2021 Handbook)

A third-year zoology subject listed in the university's 2021 Handbook. A route for studying evolution from a biological sciences base.

Glossary

Acheulean
A stone-tool tradition, beginning about 1.76 million years ago, known for symmetrical handaxes shaped on both faces.
Ancient DNA (aDNA)
Genetic material recovered from archaeological or fossil remains. It is usually fragmented and needs special methods to avoid contamination.
Ancient proteins (palaeoproteomics)
The study of proteins, such as those in tooth enamel, that survive longer than DNA. Used to identify fossils such as the Xiahe and Harbin Denisovans.
Australopith
An informal term for Australopithecus and related early hominins, including Paranthropus.
Bipedalism
Habitually walking upright on two legs.
Denisovans
An extinct human group related to Neanderthals, first identified from DNA in 2010.
Hominin
Humans and all extinct species more closely related to us than to chimpanzees.
ka / Ma
Thousands of years ago (kilo-annum) and millions of years ago (mega-annum).
Luminescence dating (OSL)
A method that measures when mineral grains were last exposed to sunlight, used to date sediments such as those at Madjedbebe.
Mosaic evolution
Different body parts and behaviours evolving at different rates, rather than as a package.
Oldowan
The earliest widespread stone-tool tradition: simple flakes and cores, from about 2.6 million years ago or earlier.
Repatriation
The return of ancestral remains and cultural material to their communities of origin.
Sahul
The ice-age continent formed by Australia, New Guinea and Tasmania when sea levels were lower.
Sunda
The ice-age extension of mainland Southeast Asia that joined Sumatra, Java and Borneo to the continent.
Uranium-series dating
Dating based on the decay of uranium to thorium in calcite or teeth. Used to give minimum ages for rock art covered by calcite crusts.
Wallacea
The zone of islands between Sunda and Sahul, separated by deep water, including Sulawesi, Flores and Timor.
