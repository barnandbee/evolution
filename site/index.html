Human Origins: seven million years in four modules

How did a lineage of African apes become a single species living on every continent? This course covers the fossil, archaeological and genetic evidence, with a running focus on the research coming out of Australia and our region.

Acknowledgement of Country

We acknowledge the Traditional Owners and Custodians of the lands, waters and skies across Australia, and pay our respects to Elders past and present. Much of the evidence discussed in Module 4 comes from their Country, and is studied with their permission and partnership.

Cultural notice

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander readers are advised that this course names and discusses ancestral remains and people who have died, including the ancestors known as Mungo Lady and Mungo Man. No images of human remains are shown.

What you will be able to do

  • Describe the main stages of human evolution and the species that represent them.
  • Explain how scientists date fossils and artefacts, and why dates get revised.
  • Read a news story about a new fossil and judge how strong the claim is.
  • Explain why Australia and Island Southeast Asia are central to current research.
  • Discuss the ethics of studying ancestral remains and working with First Nations communities.

A note on dates and uncertainty

Dates in this course are approximate and are rounded from published studies. "Ma" means millions of years ago and "ka" means thousands of years ago. Human evolution is an active field: a single new fossil or genome can move a date by hundreds of thousands of years or redraw a branch of the family tree. Where researchers disagree, the course says so and sets out the competing views.
